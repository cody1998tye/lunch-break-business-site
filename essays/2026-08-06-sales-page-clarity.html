Sales Page Clarity

When it takes you a week to write the sales page, clarity is the bottleneck.

The first time I sold something, the page didn't exist yet. Eleven people had already paid me before I opened the doc.

Writing is a skill. Pages can be badly written. That much is true. It just isn't the reason a page takes a week.

A sales page is downstream of a decision. The decision is one problem, one person. Once you've made it, the rest of the page is bookkeeping. Headline, bullets, price, guarantee. That stuff writes itself.

The eleven sales happened because I'd been making the promise out loud for months. In DMs. In comments. In conversations. By the time I sat down to write the page, the sales conversation was already written. The page just captured what I was already saying.

The week you spend not-writing is the week you spend not-deciding. The doc stays blank because the decision hasn't been made. The page can't resolve what you haven't chosen. Every time you open the tab and stare at it, you're negotiating with yourself about who this is for and what problem you're solving. That negotiation has to end before the writing can start.

Make the decision. Open the doc. Know who, know what. Finish it in an evening.

The page stops being the mountain. It becomes the transcript of a decision you already made.
