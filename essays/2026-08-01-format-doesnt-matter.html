Three Reasons Most 9-5 Workers Never Finish Their First Offer

Sometimes we sit down to build the thing and don't build anything. We're stuck deciding how to package what we know before we've even written it down. PDF. Community. App. Templates. The loop runs until we close the laptop.

1. The loop is happening before there's anything to format.

The format decision is happening before anything exists to format. We're comparing PDF vs. community vs. course vs. template and haven't written a single line of what we actually know yet.

The container is getting all the attention. The contents are still sitting in our heads.

You can't package something that doesn't exist. The first move isn't picking a format. It's writing down what you know and getting it to someone who needs it.

2. They believe the format determines whether people will buy.

The belief underneath the loop is that format is what makes people buy. A community feels more valuable than a PDF. So we stay in the loop deciding which one sends the right signal.

Buying decisions work differently. People buy because something solved their problem.

The format is the wrapper. The knowledge inside it is the reason they paid.

3. They're waiting for the format to feel right before starting.

This one is the quietest. We're waiting for the format to feel right before we start.

That feeling doesn't come from thinking. It comes from shipping.

The person who buys your first version will tell you what format works. That information doesn't exist until someone buys. You can't think your way to it.

Synthesis

Format is a packaging question. You answer packaging questions after you have something to package.

The loop that stops most first offers is an energy problem. Everything goes to the container. Nothing gets written down.

Close

Build the simplest version of what you know. Ship it. The format finds you after that.
