Chapter 4 of 8 · The Lunch Break Blueprint

PACKAGE IT

You have your Overlap and your transformation sentence. Now you turn it into something a stranger can understand and say yes to in under a minute: one small offer, one clear result, one price.

Why your first offer should be small

Your first offer has three jobs:

  1. Prove someone will pay you.
  2. Get you a result you can point to.
  3. Get finished.

A big offer fails all three. A 12-module course takes months to build, scares buyers off with the price, and drops you right back into the Chapter 1 loop. A small offer you can build in a week or two of lunch breaks gets you to $500 and gives you something to build on.

In Chapter 6 you'll sell this before you build it, so keep it small enough that you'd be comfortable delivering it within two weeks of your first sale.

Pick a shape

Most first offers fit one of three shapes:

Shape Example Typical first price Sales to hit $500 Build time on lunch breaks
Guide or playbook A step-by-step PDF or Notion page $19 to $49 11 to 27 1 to 2 weeks
Toolkit Templates, scripts, checklists, a spreadsheet $9 to $29 18 to 56 A few days to a week
Service An audit, a review, a done-for-you fix $50 to $200 3 to 10 Per buyer

A guide or toolkit is built once and sold over and over. A service needs fewer buyers, but every sale costs you time. With 30 minutes a day, either works. Pick the one that matches how fast your people want the result.

I've sold both. My Threads playbook is a guide. My Threads Visibility Audit is a service where I go through someone's account and tell them exactly what to fix.

One clear result

The result is the promise your offer makes. A good one is:

  • Specific. "Get your first 1,000 followers" beats "grow on social media."
  • Something they'd say out loud. Use their words, the ones from your transformation sentence.
  • Something you can actually deliver. You control the steps you teach. You don't control the economy, the algorithm, or whether they do the work.

Write it with this formula:

"[Who] gets [result] [in what time or with what limit] without [the thing they hate]."

Example: "Homeowners get the 12 questions that keep a contractor honest, in one page, without spending hours on YouTube."

Name it plainly

A clear name beats a clever one. The name should tell a stranger what it is and what it does. My first offer was called the 30-Minute Visibility System. You know the time it takes and the result before you read a word of the sales page.

A few patterns that work: The [Result] [Thing] / [Number] [Things] for [Result] / [Time] [Result] [System]

Pick one price

One offer, one price. No tiers, no "basic and premium." Tiers make people think instead of buy, and they make you build three things.

  • Price the result. If your guide saves someone $800 on a furnace, $29 is cheap. Page count doesn't matter.
  • Make the $500 math work. Divide 500 by your price. That's how many sales you need.
  • Pick a number you can say without flinching. If you can't type the price without cringing, you'll undersell it in every post.
  • You can raise it later. My first offer started at $17. Once it had sold and I had results to show, I raised it to $29 and then to $49.

How I built my first offer

"When I built my first offer it was very far from something special. I knew what my system was and how I did it and also how I wanted to teach it to others. So what I did was talked back and forth with Claude — it asked me questions, I answered the way I'd explain it to a guy at work, and we shaped my answers into a plain document. My first version was just a plain Word doc. No templates, no design. I have updated and made lots of changes over time, but your first version needs to be done and validated. The changes and updates can be made later." — Cody

What goes inside

Only what gets them the result faster. For a first offer, that's usually:

  • The steps. The exact path from where they are to the result.
  • One tool. A template, script or checklist that does the hardest step for them.
  • One example. Yours, filled in, so they can see what done looks like.

If an idea doesn't make the result faster or easier, save it for version two.

Add a simple guarantee

Since you're selling before you build, take the risk off the buyer: "If I don't deliver by [date], you get a full refund." It costs you nothing if you finish, and it makes saying yes easy. It also gives you a deadline you can't wiggle out of.

Common traps

  • Building a course. Video modules, a community, bonuses. Save it for later. Your first offer should be the smallest thing that gets the result.
  • Pricing from fear. $5 tells people it's worth $5, and you'd need 100 sales.
  • Promising what you can't control. "Make $10K a month" is a refund request waiting to happen. Promise the steps and the result you can stand behind.
  • Polishing the name for a week. Pick a clear one in five minutes. You can rename it after it sells.

Test it. Text your one-line pitch to one of the three people you named in Chapter 3. Ask: "Would this be worth $[price] to you? What would make it a no-brainer?" Write down exactly what they say. You'll use their words in Chapter 6.

Do it with AI — copy this prompt into ChatGPT or Claude
You're my offer coach. You're walking me through "Build your Offer Card" from Chapter 4 of Cody Tye's guide "Your First $500 Online." The goal: turn my Overlap into ONE small offer with one clear result, one plain name and one price, that I can build in about two weeks of 30-minute lunch breaks and use to make my first $500.

About me:
- My transformation sentence from Chapter 3: [I help ___ go from ___ to ___ using what I know about ___]
- Who I pictured buying it (the 3 people I named): [example: my cousin, two guys from work]
- How much time I have each day: [example: 30 minutes on lunch]

How to run this:
1. Ask me ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer before you ask the next one.
2. Ask who exactly it's for and what problem they have RIGHT NOW.
3. Help me pick a shape: guide/playbook, toolkit, or service.
4. Help me write the result: "[Who] gets [result] [in what time or with what limit] without [the thing they hate]."
5. Help me decide what goes inside. Max 3 to 5 items. Cut anything that doesn't make the result faster.
6. Give me 3 plain name options. Let me pick or tweak one.
7. Help me pick ONE price (no tiers). Show me the $500 math.
8. Ask how long delivery will take and help me write a simple guarantee.
9. Give me my feedback:
   - My finished Offer Card
   - My one-line pitch: "[Name] helps [who] [get what result] for $[price]."
   - The weakest part of this offer and how to fix it
   - A short text I can send to one real person to test the offer

Rules:
- Keep every message short. I'm reading on my phone on a break.
- Be direct and honest. Don't flatter me.
- Keep the offer SMALL. Stop me if I start adding modules or bonuses.
