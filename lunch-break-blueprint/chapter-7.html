Chapter 7 of 8 · The Lunch Break Blueprint

DELIVER IT AND FINISH

People paid you. Now you build exactly what you sold, get it into their hands on time, and turn their results into proof for your next sale. This is the part most people never reach, and it's the part that changes how you see yourself. Finished beats polished.

Build only what you sold

Open your sales page and look at the "What you get" section. That's your build list. Nothing more.

Then look at what your buyers told you when you asked about their #1 problem. Whatever came up most goes first in the offer.

Every new idea you get while building goes on a "version two" list. You can add it later, after you've delivered.

Work backward from your delivery date

Take your delivery date and subtract two days. That's your real deadline. The two days are for the stuff you didn't see coming: a sick kid, overtime, a dead phone.

Now break the build into pieces that each fit in one session. For a guide, it usually looks like this:

PieceSessions
Outline every section1
Write each section1 per section
Build your one tool (template, script, checklist)1 to 2
Fill in your example1
Read it start to finish and fix what's unclear1
Format it and upload it1

Count your sessions and put each one on a day. If they don't fit before your real deadline, cut something from the build or use your backup window from Chapter 2.

Build it by talking

You don't have to sit and type for hours. I built my first offer by going back and forth with Claude: it asked me questions, I answered the way I'd explain it to a guy at work, and we shaped my answers into a plain document.

You can do the same with voice-to-text on your breaks. Talk through one section at a time. Let the AI organize it, then fix anything that doesn't sound like you. The AI can arrange your words, but the stories, the mistakes and the know-how have to come from you. That's what people paid for.

Know when it's done

Your offer is done when it passes these five checks:

  1. It delivers every item on your sales page.
  2. A buyer could follow it without having to message you.
  3. One person you trust read it and understood it.
  4. Every link works.
  5. It's in a format they can open on their phone.

A fancy design, a logo or custom fonts aren't on that list. Mine was a plain document, and people got results from it.

If you pass the five checks and still want to keep tweaking, notice what's happening. A finished thing can be judged, and polishing is a comfortable way to keep it unfinished. Ship it on or before your date.

Deliver it

Send it however your checkout platform delivers files, or by email with a link. Either way, send a short delivery message with it:

"Hey [name], [offer name] is ready. Here's your link: [link] Start with [the first section or step]. It'll take about [time]. I'll check in on [day] to see how it's going. If you get stuck before then, reply here."

If you're delivering early, say so. It's one of the best feelings in this whole process, for you and for them.

Follow up and collect proof

Delivering is half the job. The other half is making sure people use it, because their results become your proof.

  • Day 3: Check in. "Have you had a chance to start? Where are you stuck?" Help anyone who's stuck.
  • Day 7 to 10: Ask for feedback. Ask specific questions so you get a real answer: Where were you before you got this? What did you do with it? What happened? What would you add or change? Can I share what you said?

Screenshot every kind message and every result. Always ask before you share someone's words, and never polish them into something they didn't say.

What good proof looks like

Here are two of the first testimonials I got for my offer:

"I shifted from mostly posting to engaging in the comments and I started meeting people, making real connections. As a dad who works 10–12h/day, I needed something sustainable and that's exactly what you created." — Marcos Coppa, Founder, Evolve Business. He went from 200 to over 1K followers in less than 4 weeks.
"What he's put together isn't theory. It's a documented pattern that actually compounds. Deep enough to actually work." — Hunter Glassie, Founder, Flourish Networking

Look at what makes Marcos's so strong. It has a before (200 followers, working 10 to 12 hours a day), what he did, and an after (over 1K in under four weeks). The five feedback questions are built to pull that structure out.

If you're going to miss your date

Tell your buyers before the date, never after. Say what happened, give a new date you can hit, and offer a full refund to anyone who'd rather not wait. Most people will wait, and they'll trust you more for being straight with them.

Celebrate the finish

Post about it. "Delivered [offer name] to all [number] buyers today, two days early." It's a win you earned, it shows people you finish what you sell, and it sets up your next round of sales in Chapter 8.

Think back to your Restart Audit from Chapter 1. This might be the first thing on that list you actually finished. That counts for more than the money.

Common traps

  • Adding bonuses nobody asked for. Every extra pushes your date. Put it on the version two list.
  • Redesigning instead of finishing. Plain and done beats pretty and late.
  • Going quiet on buyers while you build. A quick update halfway through keeps people excited.
  • Delivering and disappearing. The check-in and feedback are where your next sales come from.

Do it with AI — copy this prompt into ChatGPT or Claude
You're my build partner. You're walking me through "Build it, deliver it, follow up" from Chapter 7 of Cody Tye's guide "Your First $500 Online." The goal: build exactly what I sold, nothing more, deliver it on or before my date, and follow up with buyers to collect feedback and proof. Finished beats polished.

About me:
- My offer name and shape (guide, toolkit or service): [example: The Contractor Cheat Sheet, a guide]
- The "What you get" list from my sales page: [paste it]
- What my buyers said they want help with most: [paste their answers]
- My delivery date: [date]
- When I work on it each day: [example: 30 minutes at lunch, 30 minutes after the kids are asleep]

How to run this:
1. Ask me ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer before you ask the next one.
2. BUILD LIST: Turn my "What you get" list into a build list. Put what buyers want most first. Anything extra goes on a "version two" list.
3. SCHEDULE: Set my real deadline two days before delivery. Break into pieces that fit one session each.
4. BUILD: Interview me with short questions to pull out what I know. Draft each section from my answers, in the way I talk. Don't invent experience or stories I didn't give you.
5. TOOL AND EXAMPLE: Help me build my one tool and fill in one example using my own situation.
6. FIVE CHECKS: Walk me through the five checks. If I keep wanting to tweak after it passes, tell me to ship.
7. DELIVERY AND FOLLOW-UP: Write my delivery message, Day 3 check-in, and Day 7 feedback request.
8. Give me my feedback:
   - My build list and version two list
   - My day-by-day schedule
   - My delivery message, check-in and feedback request, ready to copy
   - A short "delivered" post I can share when it's done

Rules:
- Keep every message short. I'm reading on my phone on a break.
- Be direct and honest.
- Keep it simple. Push back on bonuses and redesigns.
- Never make up testimonials, results or stories.
