DO IT AGAIN

You sold something, built it and delivered it. Most people who start never get that far. The first $500 proves the thing works. What you do next is what turns it into a business. The fastest way to your next $500 is the offer you already have.

Look back before you move on

The US Army uses something called an after-action review. After every mission, they answer four plain questions. They work just as well for a first offer:

  1. What was supposed to happen?
  2. What actually happened?
  3. Why was there a difference?
  4. What will I do differently next time?

Answer them honestly, in writing. Look at where your sales came from, what buyers said, and what took longer than you planned. This takes one session, and it'll save you weeks.

Sell the same thing again

Your instinct will be to make something new. Don't. You already have an offer with buyers, results and testimonials. That's worth more than any new idea.

"My first offer pre-sold 11 copies. I kept selling that same offer, improved it and renamed it, and it's sold about 70 times now. Most of those later sales came without a single DM, from people clicking the link in my bio or on my posts." — Cody

To get the same offer selling on its own:

  • Keep the link up. Put it in your bio and on a pinned post. Switch the sales page from pre-sale to "available now."
  • Add your proof. Put your best testimonials on the sales page, with permission.
  • Keep mentioning it. At least once a day, write a post that points to it: a buyer's result, a lesson from inside, a question someone asked.
  • Keep your daily 30 minutes. The replies and posts from Chapter 5 are what keep new people finding you.

Make version two

Remember the version two list from Chapter 7? Pick the two or three things buyers asked for most and add them. Fix anything people got stuck on. Give the update to everyone who already bought, free. It keeps your word and turns early buyers into your biggest fans.

Raise your price when the proof backs it up

I started at $17, then $29, and now $49. Each raise came after the offer had more buyers, more results and more inside it.

A good rule: when you have real testimonials and sales come in without you pushing, try a higher price. If sales hold, keep it. If they stop cold, you learned something, and you can go back.

Let buyers tell you the next offer

The best second offer is usually sitting in your DMs. Pay attention to what buyers ask for after they finish:

  • "Can you just look at mine and tell me what's wrong?" That's a service.
  • "Do you have a template for this part?" That's a toolkit.
  • "Can you help me with the next step?" That's your next guide, or coaching.

When the same request shows up from three or more people, you've found it. Then run the same playbook.

"I learned this the hard way. My Threads audits came from buyers asking me to look at their accounts. Later, I spent five or six hours building an AI tool I was sure my audience needed, launched it, and nobody wanted it. I built what I thought they needed instead of what they were asking for. Don't make my mistake." — Cody

Common traps

  • Starting over with a new idea. Your first offer still has sales left in it. Keep selling it.
  • Building the next offer before anyone asks. Wait until you hear the same request three times.
  • Dropping your daily 30 minutes. When the posts and replies stop, the sales slow down.
  • Never raising your price. If your proof has grown, your price can too.
