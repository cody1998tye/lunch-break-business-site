SELL IT BEFORE YOU BUILD IT

This is the chapter that gets you paid. You're going to put your offer up for sale before it exists, collect orders, and build it for the people who bought. The cook doesn't start the burger until the ticket comes in. Your offer works the same way.

Why sell first

  • You find out if people will pay before you spend weeks building. If nobody buys, you lost a few days instead of a few months.
  • Your buyers tell you what to build. You'll ask them what they need most and make exactly that.
  • You get a deadline you can't wiggle out of. People paid you. You'll finish. This is the fix for the Chapter 1 loop.

How it went for me

I learned the sell-first idea from a course, from a guy named Adam Dukes. The course was called "The 3 Hour Creator."

I was scared to try it. My biggest worry was that nobody would pay for something they couldn't have right away.

"I did it anyway. I put up a sales page and a buy link for the 30-Minute Visibility System at $9, announced it, and gave myself 11 days from that first post. Eleven people bought it. I built it by going back and forth with Claude, turned it into a plain document, and delivered it to all eleven buyers two days early. Here's one of my launch posts: '6 days until The 30-Minute Visibility System launches. Go from Invisible → Visible on Threads with my 30 minute a day System. March 14th. Mark it.' Short, a clear before and after, and a date." — Cody

The sales came in as a trickle, about one a day. Most of those first 11 came from DMs with people I'd built trust with in the months before the launch. Only a couple came from people buying straight off a post.

That's changed since. The offer has sold about 70 times now, and most of those sales come from people buying through the link in my bio or on my posts, with no DM at all. Your first sales come from the trust you've already built. Once the offer has buyers and results behind it, your posts start doing more of the selling.

Is this legit?

Yes, as long as you're honest. Pre-orders are everywhere: books, video games, Kickstarter. What makes it fair:

  • Say clearly that it's a pre-sale and when they'll get it.
  • Give a delivery date you can hit.
  • Offer a full refund if you don't deliver on time.
  • Only pre-sell something you can actually build in that window.

The five parts

  1. A one-page sales page. Simple. You'll write it below.
  2. A buy link. Any simple checkout works. I use Followrs. Gumroad, Stan Store or a Stripe payment link work too.
  3. An announcement post. The sales page and buy link go live the same moment you post it.
  4. A sales window. About 11 days of posting about your offer every day.
  5. A delivery date and guarantee. Written on the page, with a refund if you miss it.

Tell the people who already talk to you

Go through the conversations you started with new followers in Chapter 5. Send each one a personal message and ask before you send a link:

"Hey [name], you mentioned you were stuck on [their problem]. I just opened a pre-sale for [offer name], which walks through exactly that. It's $[price] until [date]. Want the link?"

These people already know you. Most of my first 11 sales came from conversations like these.

When someone buys

Message them the same day:

"Thanks for grabbing [offer name]. It'll be in your inbox by [date]. Quick question so I make it as useful as possible: what's the #1 thing you want help with?"

Their answers tell you what to put first.

If nobody's buying

After four days with zero sales, look at where it's breaking:

  1. Are people seeing it? Check views. If they're low, keep up the replies and post at better times.
  2. Are they clicking? If people see it but don't click, your post isn't making the problem clear enough.
  3. Are they clicking but not buying? Fix the headline, the who-it's-for, or the price on your page.
  4. Ask. Message a few people who know you: "What would make this a yes for you?"

If you finish the window with no sales, that's a cheap answer. You found out in 11 days instead of 11 months. Go back to Chapter 3 and test your next Overlap.

Common traps

  • Building it "just a little" first. Put the page up first. Build after the first sale.
  • Announcing once and going quiet. One post reaches a small slice of your followers. Post about it every day of the window.
  • Apologizing for selling. You're offering help you know works. Say what it does and what it costs.
  • Picking a delivery date you can't hit. Give yourself extra days. Delivering early beats delivering late every time.

Step 6. Write your buyer thank-you message.

Do it with AI — copy this prompt into ChatGPT or Claude
You're my launch coach. You're walking me through The Order Ticket from Chapter 6 of Cody Tye's guide "Your First $500 Online." The goal: pre-sell my offer before I build it. I'll put up a simple one-page sales page and buy link, announce it, post about it every day for about 11 days, and deliver to buyers by a date I can hit, with a full refund if I don't.

About me:
- My Offer Card from Chapter 4 (name, who it's for, problem, result, what's inside, price, guarantee): [paste it]
- What my test person said about the offer: [paste their words]
- My one line of proof: [example: 10 years in HVAC]
- How many days I need to build it: [example: 10 days of lunch breaks]
- The platform I post on: [example: Threads]

How to run this:
1. Ask me ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer before you ask the next one.
2. Set my delivery date. Ask how long building will really take, then add buffer days.
3. SALES PAGE: Write it section by section: headline, who it's for, the problem, what they get, when they get it (pre-sale with date), price, guarantee, one line about me, buy button text.
4. ANNOUNCEMENT: Help me write my announcement post.
5. 11-DAY PLAN: Write 10 more sales posts using these angles: the problem, why I made it, who it's for and who it isn't, behind the scenes, teach one thing, answer an objection, proof, a story, deadline coming, last call.
6. WARM MESSAGES: Help me write a short personal message for people who already know me.
7. BUYER THANK-YOU: Write a message I send the day someone buys.
8. Give me my feedback:
   - My full sales page, ready to copy
   - My announcement post
   - All 10 sales posts, labeled Day 2 to Day 11
   - My warm message and buyer thank-you
   - What to check if I have zero sales after 4 days

Rules:
- Keep every message short. I'm reading on my phone on a break.
- Be direct and honest.
- Be honest that it's a pre-sale everywhere. No fake scarcity.
- Don't let me start building before the page is live.
